## When Do You Call a Restoration Company Instead of a Roofer or Plumber?

Call a restoration company first whenever wind or water has breached the building envelope and the inside of the home is wet, not just the outside. A roofer alone can patch shingles, but if rain is already running down an interior wall or pooling on a floor, that's a drying and documentation job as much as a repair job.

Call 911 or your utility company first, before anyone else, if you see a downed power line, smell gas, or the structure looks unsafe to approach. No restoration company should be the first call when there's an active life-safety hazard.

Call a roofer or handyman directly, and skip the restoration step, only when the damage is purely exterior: a few missing shingles, a dented gutter, no interior leak at all. Once water has actually entered the structure, from a wind-torn roof, a cracked window, or storm surge pushing in at ground level, you're better off with a company that handles both the emergency stabilization (tarping, board-up, extraction) and the insurance documentation in one visit, which is what [HomeLyft](/services/storm-damage-restoration/) does for Gulfport homeowners.

## Is Wind Damage Covered Differently Than Flood Damage on the Coast?

Yes. Wind and hail damage to a roof or windows is typically handled under your standard homeowners policy, while rising water from storm surge, a overflowing bayou, or surface flooding is only covered by a separate flood insurance policy, usually through the [National Flood Insurance Program](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance).

This split matters because a single storm on the Mississippi coast often causes both kinds of damage at once: wind rips off roof decking in one event, and surge or heavy rain floods the first floor in the same event. Most homeowners policies exclude flood entirely, so the water that came up from the ground gets adjusted differently than the water that came down through the roof. If you're not sure which applies, our [flood damage restoration](/services/flood-damage-restoration/) and [water damage restoration](/services/water-damage-restoration/) teams can separate the two sources during the initial assessment so each claim goes to the right policy. ## What Should You Do in the First Few Hours After Storm Damage?

Secure the home and document everything before you start cleaning up. Photos of the damage as it actually happened protect the claim far more than a verbal description after the fact.

Start with these steps:

1. **Check for hazards first.** Gas smell, exposed wiring, or standing water near an electrical panel means stay out and call your utility or the fire department.
2. **Photograph the damage from multiple angles**, including the exterior breach (the hole in the roof, the broken window) and the interior result (the wet ceiling, the pooled water).
3. **Stop further water intrusion.** A tarp over a roof hole or plywood over a broken window keeps the next rain band from doubling the damage. [FEMA and Ready.gov](https://www.ready.gov/hurricanes) recommend this as a core post-storm action, and it's exactly what our [emergency board-up and tarping](/services/emergency-board-up-tarping/) crews handle.
4. **Call your insurer after mitigation starts, not before.** Most policies require you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, and a restoration company's scope and photos give the adjuster something concrete to work from.

Our [water damage insurance claim tips](/blog/water-damage-insurance-claim-tips/) post goes deeper on what to photograph and what to keep for the adjuster.

## What Happens When You Call a Storm Damage Restoration Company?

The first call establishes what's actually wet or breached, then schedules a technician to assess, stabilize, and document the loss. Have your address, a rough description of the damage (roof, windows, water intrusion), and your insurance information ready.

On arrival, the crew typically inspects the full extent of the breach, not just the visible damage, since wind-driven rain often tracks further into wall cavities and attic spaces than it looks from inside a room. They install emergency tarping or board-up to stop ongoing intrusion, extract any standing water, and set moisture-mapping readings on affected materials. Everything gets photographed and logged into a written scope before any demolition or drying equipment goes in, which is the documentation your adjuster will ask for. If the water sat for more than a day or two, expect a conversation about [mold remediation](/services/storm-damage-restoration/) risk as part of the same visit, since damp drywall and insulation can start supporting mold growth within 24 to 48 hours according to the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold).
