## Who do I call first after a tree falls on my house in Gulfport?

Call 911 first only if someone is hurt or a power line is down, sparking, or touching the tree. Otherwise, your first calls are Mississippi Power (for any downed or damaged line near the tree) and your homeowners insurance company, in that order, before you touch anything.

Once the property is confirmed safe and your insurer has your claim number, call a restoration company that handles emergency tarping and board-up, not a general tree service. HomeLyft Restoration Gulfport answers 24/7 at [(228) 284-5200](tel:+12282845200) and can get a tarp on the roof and the structure secured the same day, which matters more than almost anything else in the first 24 hours after a tree comes through a roof or wall.

Don't climb on the roof yourself. A tree impact often cracks rafters or trusses that aren't visible from the attic, and Gulf Coast storms rarely drop just one hazard at a time, downed limbs, wet shingles, and loose decking make a roof walk genuinely dangerous even for someone experienced with ladders.

## Should I call a tree removal company or a restoration company first?

Call the restoration company first. Tree removal and structural stabilization are two different jobs, and the stabilization has to happen first or the house keeps taking on damage while you wait for a tree crew's schedule to open up.

HomeLyft Restoration Gulfport does not perform standalone tree removal, but the crew coordinates directly with licensed tree services to get the trunk and limbs off the structure safely, then handles everything the tree leaves behind: emergency tarping, board-up of broken windows or walls, moisture assessment if rain got into the attic or wall cavity, and the roofing or reconstruction work to put the house back together. That single point of contact matters when you're also juggling an insurance adjuster, a tree crew, and a roofer, you want one company tracking the whole timeline instead of three separate businesses that don't talk to each other.

If the tree is small and already off the roof, you may be able to skip the separate tree service and let the restoration crew clear light debris as part of the tarping visit. For a mature oak or pine still resting on the structure, you'll likely need both a tree crew for the removal and a restoration crew for everything the impact exposed.

## What happens when the restoration crew arrives?

The crew's first job is stopping further damage, not starting repairs. That means a structural walk-through, a tarp or plywood over any opening, and photo documentation before anything gets moved.

A technician will assess where the tree penetrated the roof deck or wall, check the attic and ceiling below for active leaks, and photograph the damage from multiple angles for your claim file. If rain is already getting in, expect a heavy-duty tarp secured with batten strips rather than a quick plastic sheet. Any broken windows, doors, or exterior wall openings get boarded with plywood the same visit.

From there, the scope splits into phases: emergency mitigation (tarping, board-up, water extraction if needed) happens within hours, a detailed damage assessment and insurance estimate follows within a day or two, and the actual roofing or reconstruction work, covered under the client's [roofing](/services/roofing/) and [reconstruction](/services/reconstruction/) services, gets scheduled once the claim is approved. ## Will insurance cover a tree falling on my house?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden tree-impact damage to the dwelling and sometimes the cost of removing the tree from the structure, but check your policy's wording and limits before assuming full coverage. Mississippi policies vary by insurer, and the [Mississippi Insurance Department](https://www.mid.ms.gov/) is the state resource for coverage questions or complaints.

In general, if the tree was healthy and fell because of wind, the dwelling damage is typically covered under the wind or storm peril section of your policy. If the tree was already dead, diseased, or leaning for months before it fell, some insurers push back on the claim as a maintenance issue, so document the tree's prior condition with any photos you have. The [Insurance Information Institute's guide to disaster coverage](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance) breaks down how wind and storm perils are typically handled. Your deductible still applies, and most Mississippi coastal policies carry a separate, higher wind/hurricane deductible than the standard one, so ask your adjuster which deductible applies to this specific loss.

Document everything before cleanup starts: photos of the tree, the point of impact, and any interior damage, plus the date and approximate time if you know it. The [water damage insurance claim tips](/blog/water-damage-insurance-claim-tips/) post covers documentation habits that apply just as well to a tree-impact claim.

Once the roof is tarped and the claim is moving, the scramble is over. The next decision is simply choosing when the permanent roof or wall repair gets scheduled, and that's a conversation worth having with a licensed crew rather than rushing into with the first available contractor.
