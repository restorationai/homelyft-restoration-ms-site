## Do You Need a Reconstruction Company or a Different Kind of Contractor?

You need a reconstruction company when the rebuild follows storm, fire, or water damage and has to match an approved insurance scope. You need a different call entirely if water is still running, wires are live, or you smell gas.

If the loss is still active, active meaning standing water rising, sparking outlets, or a gas odor, call 911 or your utility first. Reconstruction is the second call, made once the hazard is controlled and mitigation (drying, demo, debris removal) is underway or finished.

Once the structure is stable, reconstruction is the right call if you're missing framing, subfloor, drywall, or roof decking that an insurance adjuster has already scoped. A company that also handles [water damage restoration](/services/water-damage-restoration/), [storm damage restoration](/services/storm-damage-restoration/), and [fire damage restoration](/services/fire-damage-restoration/) under one roof closes that gap faster, because there's no handoff between the drying crew and the rebuild crew. If the work isn't tied to a covered loss at all, say you just want a bathroom updated, that's a standalone [general contracting](/services/general-contracting/) job, not an insurance-driven reconstruction scope, though the same crew often handles both.

Either way, loop your insurer in once mitigation starts, not before you've documented the damage. Our guide on [documenting a water damage claim](/blog/water-damage-insurance-claim-tips/) covers what photos and records protect a reconstruction scope later.

## What Happens When You Call for Reconstruction Help in Gulfport?

The first call gets a scope visit scheduled, usually to measure the damage and compare it against what the insurance adjuster has already approved, before any lumber moves.

Have three things ready when you call: the adjuster's written scope of work or claim number if one exists, the mitigation report from whoever dried or demoed the structure, and any photos taken right after the loss. These keep the reconstruction estimate tied to what's actually covered instead of starting from scratch.

On-site, the crew measures affected framing, subfloor, drywall, and roofline, and checks the rebuild against the baseline codes Mississippi municipalities adopt from the [International Code Council](https://www.iccsafe.org/). Structural work in Gulfport typically needs a building permit pulled before framing starts; a reconstruction company should handle that paperwork rather than leave it to the homeowner.

Timeline varies with scope. A single damaged room usually runs two to four weeks once materials are picked. A whole-structure rebuild after major storm damage can take several months depending on permitting backlog and material availability. Once the scope is agreed on, work typically moves room by room, rough framing and inspections first, then drywall, paint, flooring, and trim, with a final walkthrough before the job closes out.
