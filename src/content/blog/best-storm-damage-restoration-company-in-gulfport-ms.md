## What does a storm damage restoration company actually do after a hurricane or tropical storm?

A storm damage restoration job runs through three phases: stabilize, dry, rebuild. Each phase has to happen in order or the next one fails.

1. **Emergency stabilization** [emergency board-up and tarping](/services/emergency-board-up-tarping/) within hours, covering broken windows, exposed roof sections, and compromised siding so wind-driven rain stops entering the structure.
2. **Water mitigation and drying.** Once the structure is sealed, extraction and structural drying begin, following the [ANSI/IICRC S500](https://iicrc.org/s500/) standard for water damage restoration. This step catches hidden moisture in wall cavities and subfloors before mold has a chance to establish, which the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold) notes can start within 24 to 48 hours on wet materials.
3. **Roofing and reconstruction.** Once the structure is dry, [roofing](/services/roofing/) repairs or replacement happen, often to the Fortified standard for better wind resistance going forward, followed by [reconstruction](/services/reconstruction/) of drywall, flooring, and finishes.

Skipping a step, especially drying before rebuilding, is the most common reason storm repairs end up with mold problems six months later. ## How do I get insurance to cover storm damage repairs?

Most homeowners policies cover wind damage from a named storm, hurricane, or straight-line wind event, but coverage splits sharply from there. Wind and wind-driven rain through a damaged roof or broken window is typically a covered peril under a standard policy, while storm surge and rising flood water require separate flood insurance, since standard homeowners policies exclude flood ([Triple-I](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance)).

Document everything before cleanup starts: photos of the damage, the tarp or board-up invoice, and a written scope of work. A company that photographs and logs damage as part of the mitigation call makes the claim easier to support later. Always check your specific policy; wind/hail deductibles on the Mississippi coast often run as a separate percentage deductible rather than a flat dollar amount.
