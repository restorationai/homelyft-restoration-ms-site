## How Does Water Damage Restoration Actually Work?

Water damage restoration runs through four phases: inspecting and categorizing the water, extracting what's standing, drying the structure to a verified dry standard, and repairing or replacing damaged materials. A straightforward Category 1 (clean water) loss in one room typically clears those four phases in three to five days.

The categorization step matters more than most homeowners realize. Category 1 water comes from a clean source like a supply line. Category 2, called gray water, comes from appliances like a dishwasher or washing machine. Category 3, or black water, includes sewage backups and storm floodwater, both common after heavy Gulf Coast rain events, and requires different PPE and disposal procedures under the [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org).

Extraction uses truck-mounted or portable pumps to pull out standing water, followed by moisture mapping with a penetrating meter to find what's wet behind walls and under flooring that isn't visible yet. Drying then relies on placed air movers and low-grain-refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers, monitored daily until moisture readings in the studs and subfloor return to a dry standard, not just until the floor looks and feels dry. Skipping that verification step is the single most common reason water damage reopens as a mold problem weeks later.

## What Determines How Much Water Damage Restoration Costs in Gulfport?

The size of the affected area, the category of water, and how many materials have to be removed and replaced, drywall, flooring, cabinetry, are what move the price, not the zip code. A contained one-room clean-water loss costs far less than a multi-room gray or black water loss that requires demolition.

Response time affects the number too. A leak caught and extracted within a few hours usually stays a drying job. The same leak left standing for two or three days, which happens often when a vacation rental or second home near Long Beach sits empty, often turns into a demolition and rebuild job once subfloor and drywall have absorbed water past the point of drying in place.

## What Should You Look for When Choosing a Water Damage Restoration Company?

Look for an IICRC-certified firm, a Mississippi contractor's license, direct communication with your insurance adjuster, and a written drying log, not just a verbal promise that the house is dry. Those four things separate a company that documents its work from one that guesses.

IICRC certification (the Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification) means technicians trained to the WRT (Water Damage Restoration Technician) and ASD (Applied Structural Drying) standards, which is what most insurance carriers expect to see cited in the final report. ## What Happens If Water Damage Isn't Dried Out Fast Enough?

Mold can start growing on wet drywall and framing within 24 to 48 hours, according to the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold), turning a drying job into a remediation job. In Gulfport's humid, coastal climate, that window can run even shorter during the warmer months.

Once mold is established, the job changes completely: containment barriers go up, HEPA air scrubbers run, and affected materials often need removal rather than drying. That's a different scope, different equipment, and a different cost. If you're already seeing a musty smell or discoloration on a wall that took on water days ago, that's worth a separate look; see our post on [signs of hidden mold](/blog/signs-of-hidden-mold/) for what to check before assuming it's just a drying delay.
