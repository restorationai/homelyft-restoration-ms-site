## What Does the Reconstruction Process Involve?

Reconstruction moves through assessment, documentation, demolition, and rebuild, usually in that order, with insurance coordination running alongside each phase. Skipping the documentation step is the most common reason a claim gets disputed later.

1. **Assessment and scope.** A project manager inspects the structure, identifies what is salvageable versus what needs to come out, and writes a scope of work.
2. **Insurance documentation.** Photos, moisture readings, and the written scope go to your adjuster. The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/settling-insurance-claims-after-a-disaster) notes that thorough documentation is one of the biggest factors in a smooth claim settlement.
3. **Demolition and mitigation.** Damaged drywall, flooring, and framing come out. For water or mold involvement, this follows the [ANSI/IICRC S500](https://iicrc.org/s500/) or [S520](https://iicrc.org/s520/) standard before any rebuild materials go in.
4. **Permitting.** Most structural rebuilds in Harrison County require a permit through the local building department before framing or electrical work proceeds.
5. **Rebuild.** Framing, drywall, flooring, paint, and, when the roof was part of the loss, roofing replacement under Fortified Building standards where applicable.
6. **Final walkthrough.** The homeowner signs off on the finished space before the job closes.
