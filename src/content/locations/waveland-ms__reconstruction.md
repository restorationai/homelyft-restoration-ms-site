**Reconstruction after storm or fire damage in Waveland looks different from inland rebuilds.** Much of the city went through a near-total reconstruction after Hurricane Katrina's storm surge, and that history shows up in how homes are framed, elevated, and tied to their foundations today. When fire, wind, or water damage forces another rebuild, matching that construction correctly, not just patching around it, is what keeps the next inspection and the next storm from becoming a second problem.

## Why Waveland Properties See Reconstruction Issues

Waveland sits low and close to the water, and a large share of its housing stock was rebuilt post-Katrina on elevated pilings or raised pier-and-beam foundations to meet updated flood elevation requirements. That construction style is durable, but it also means reconstruction work after a fire, roof failure, or interior flood often involves engineered connections, not simple stud-and-drywall repairs. Add in Hancock County's humidity and salt air, and exposed framing or subfloor left open too long during a rebuild can pick up moisture damage before the project is even finished. Reconstruction here has to account for both the original engineering and the coastal climate working against open structures.

## Our Reconstruction Services Process in Waveland

We start by documenting the damage against the home's existing structure, elevation certificate, and any permit history, since Hancock County inspectors will compare new work to what's on file. From there we scope the rebuild: structural framing and sheathing repair, subfloor and stair or ramp access tied back to code-required elevation, roofing and exterior envelope work, and interior finish-out. Because so many Waveland homes were built or rebuilt under the same post-storm codes, our crews are used to working with raised foundations, hurricane straps, and wind-rated connections rather than retrofitting them in after the fact. We close out with a final walkthrough so the finished work holds up to both insurance documentation and county inspection.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Reconstruction

Structural rebuild work in a wind-exposed coastal city calls for materials and fastening methods rated for that exposure, not standard inland specs. Our teams carry Fortified Building roofing and wind-storm certification into reconstruction projects, which means roof decking, fastening patterns, and flashing details are built to hold up to Gulf Coast wind events rather than just meeting a minimum code baseline. Where fire or smoke damage is part of the loss, our FSRT-trained crews handle the structural removal and prep before any rebuild framing goes back in, so new materials aren't going over residual odor or char.

## Local note

One thing that trips up reconstruction projects in Waveland: a lot of the rebuild-era homes have stairs, ramps, or landings added separately from the main structure to meet flood elevation requirements, and those additions don't always show up correctly on older permit records. When we're rebuilding after fire or storm damage, we check the elevation certificate against the actual structure first, because replacing access points without matching the certified elevation can hold up a final inspection even after the main rebuild is done right.
