**Dealing with chipping or peeling paint in a Laurel home built before 1978?** That's the line the EPA draws for lead-based paint, and in a mill town where whole blocks of workers' cottages and foursquares went up in the 1920s and 1930s, a huge share of Laurel's housing stock falls on the wrong side of it. Lead paint abatement here isn't a generic add-on service, it's a regulated process shaped by how old these homes actually are and how renovation work gets done in Jones County.

## Why Laurel Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Laurel grew up around the lumber and furniture industry, and a lot of that early growth shows up today in close-set frame houses with original wood trim, double-hung sash windows, and layered paint that's never been fully stripped. Every repaint since the 1930s just sat on top of the last one, which means the oldest, most lead-heavy coats are usually buried two or three layers down, not visible at a glance. Humid Mississippi summers make things worse: moisture gets behind window casings and porch columns, paint blisters and flakes, and that's exactly the failure mode that turns intact lead paint into airborne or ingestible dust. Renovation work, not just visible deterioration, is often what disturbs it. Someone sanding a windowsill or pulling trim to update a kitchen can release more lead dust in an afternoon than decades of normal wear.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Laurel

We start with testing, not assumptions, since paint age alone doesn't tell you lead content or condition. Once a surface tests positive, the area gets sealed off with containment barriers and negative air pressure so dust doesn't migrate into living space, which matters in Laurel's older homes where HVAC returns and floor registers often connect rooms more than modern construction would. Work follows EPA Renovation, Repair, and Painting (RRP) practices: wet methods to keep dust down, HEPA vacuum cleanup instead of dry sweeping, and verified clearance before we call an area safe to reoccupy. For homes going through a larger renovation or reconstruction project, we coordinate abatement with the rest of the scope so trim, siding, or window work doesn't get re-exposed to lead dust after the fact.

## Laurel Insurance & Renovation Coordination

Lead abatement itself isn't typically something a homeowner's insurance policy covers, since it's a renovation and compliance issue rather than storm or water loss, but it frequently comes up alongside work that is: fire or water damage repairs on an older Laurel home that trigger RRP requirements once walls or trim get opened up. When that happens, we document the lead findings separately from the covered damage so your claim paperwork and the abatement invoice don't get tangled together, which keeps both processes moving instead of stalling on confusion about what's billable to a carrier.

## Local note

One thing that catches people off guard in Laurel: original wood porch flooring and ceilings painted in the old "haint blue" tradition often carry some of the heaviest lead content in the house, because those surfaces got repainted more often than interior walls to fight humidity and insects. Homeowners restoring a porch for curb appeal sometimes sand it themselves before realizing it needs to be tested first, which is the single most common way we see lead dust get tracked into a freshly updated home.
