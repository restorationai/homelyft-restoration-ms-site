Homes here, many built on pier-and-beam or slab foundations suited to the sandy coastal soil, handle water differently than inland construction. A slab home traps water under flooring and behind baseboards where it's easy to miss; a pier-and-beam home lets water pool in the crawlspace, where it can sit unnoticed for days, feeding mold growth into the floor joists before anyone smells it upstairs.

## Why Henderson Point Properties See Water Damage Issues

The Gulf Coast's humidity and storm pattern work against fast drying. Even after extraction, ambient moisture in the air slows evaporation, so structural materials that would dry out in a drier climate in a day or two can stay damp for much longer without mechanical dehumidification. Add in coastal corrosion on older galvanized plumbing and the salt-air wear on roof flashing and window seals, and Henderson Point homes face more entry points for water than a typical inland property, whether that's a slow roof leak during a tropical system or a pinhole leak in decades-old supply lines.

## Our Emergency Water Removal Process in Henderson Point

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted and portable pumps before it has a chance to wick further into baseboards, cabinetry, and subfloor. From there we use moisture meters to map how far water has traveled behind walls and under flooring, which on a slab home often means checking further out from the visible wet line than homeowners expect. Air movers and commercial dehumidifiers go in next, calibrated for the Gulf Coast's humidity rather than a generic drying schedule, and we track readings daily until materials hit a dry standard, not just a dry appearance. Drying follows IICRC S500 guidelines throughout, since a floor that looks dry at the surface can still be saturated at the lath or subfloor level.
