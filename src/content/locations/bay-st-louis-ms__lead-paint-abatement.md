**Lead paint concerns in a Bay St. Louis home built before 1978?** It's one of the most common findings our crews run into along this stretch of the Mississippi Gulf Coast, where a huge share of the housing stock predates federal lead-based paint restrictions. Whether you're prepping for a renovation, selling a historic property, or just found peeling paint on a windowsill after storm humidity got to it, the process for handling it safely is specific, and skipping steps can mean real health risk and real liability.

## Why Bay St. Louis Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Hancock County's older neighborhoods, many rebuilt or renovated in waves after past hurricanes, still carry a lot of pre-1978 construction: raised cottages, Craftsman-style bungalows, and clapboard-sided homes that have been painted and repainted for decades. Gulf Coast humidity and salt air accelerate paint failure compared to drier inland climates, so chalking, chipping, and flaking show up faster here, especially on exterior trim, porch rails, and window sashes facing the water. Once paint starts breaking down, lead dust can settle into soil and household dust well before anyone notices visible damage, which is why testing matters even when a wall looks fine.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Bay St. Louis

We start with testing to confirm lead presence and map which surfaces are affected, since a Bay St. Louis cottage might have lead paint on the original siding but not on a porch rebuilt after a more recent storm. From there, containment goes up before any disturbance begins: plastic sheeting, negative air pressure where needed, and sealed work zones to keep dust from migrating into living space or outside soil. Removal methods are chosen based on substrate, wet scraping, chemical stripping, or encapsulation depending on whether the surface is being replaced or preserved, with HEPA vacuuming and wipe-down clearance before we consider an area closed out. Work is performed and documented following EPA Lead-Safe Certified renovation practices, which matters both for your family's safety and for resale disclosure requirements down the line.

## Local note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard here: salt humidity doesn't just make old paint fail faster, it also makes dry-scraping riskier because brittle, salt-exposed paint chips and becomes airborne more easily than paint in a drier climate.
