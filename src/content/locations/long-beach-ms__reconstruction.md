Rebuilding after a fire, storm, or major water loss in Long Beach means working with a coastal building stock that was largely replaced or elevated after Hurricane Katrina, and a current wind-and-flood code environment that doesn't forgive shortcuts. Reconstruction here isn't just framing and drywall. It's matching pier-and-beam elevations, verifying flood-zone compliance, and getting a roof installed to standards that actually hold up the next time wind comes off the Mississippi Sound.

## Why Long Beach Properties Need a Different Reconstruction Approach

Much of Long Beach sits in Harrison County's coastal flood zones, which means a meaningful share of homes were rebuilt or elevated after 2005 on piers, with modern tie-down and wind-rated roofing requirements that didn't exist on the original structures. When a house in this condition suffers fire or storm damage, reconstruction has to respect both the elevation certificate on file and current wind-load code, not just replace what was there. Older, pre-Katrina homes that survived closer to the water often have original framing paired with newer additions, which means a reconstruction crew needs to read the structure carefully before assuming one set of materials or fastening methods applies throughout.

## Our Reconstruction Services Process in Long Beach

We start with a structural assessment that separates what's salvageable from what has to come out, documenting everything for the insurance file before demolition begins. From there, our crews handle the demo, framing, electrical and plumbing rough-in coordination, drywall, and finish work needed to bring a property back, working alongside our fire damage, water damage, and storm damage teams when the reconstruction follows one of those losses. On roofing specifically, our Fortified Building certification matters in a coastal market like this one: reconstruction after wind damage is an opportunity to rebuild the roof assembly to a higher wind standard, which can also affect future insurance premiums. We coordinate permitting with Harrison County and the City of Long Beach as part of the rebuild rather than leaving that step for the homeowner to manage alone.

## Long Beach Insurance Coordination

Coastal Mississippi claims often involve more than one policy, a homeowner's policy for wind and fire damage, and separately a flood policy through the NFIP for storm surge or rising water. Reconstruction scopes have to track which damage falls under which claim, since adjusters on each side will only cover what their policy addresses. We document the loss by cause, not just by room, so the paperwork lines up with whichever carrier is paying for which part of the rebuild, and we work directly with most major carriers to keep that process from stalling.

## Local note

Homes along Long Beach's flood-zone streets frequently carry elevation certificates that specify the lowest habitable floor height. When we reconstruct after a flood or storm loss on one of these properties, we check that certificate before framing begins, because rebuilding below the certified elevation can jeopardize the homeowner's flood insurance rating going forward. It's a step that's easy to skip and expensive to get wrong.
