## Why Pascagoula Homes Still Have Lead Paint Problems

Pascagoula grew fast during the shipbuilding expansion at Ingalls, and a large share of the city's housing stock dates to the 1940s and 1950s, well before lead-based paint was restricted. Humidity off the Mississippi Sound keeps exterior paint cycling through expansion and contraction faster than it would in a drier climate, which means older coats chalk, crack, and shed dust sooner. Salt air adds to the wear on painted siding and porch rails, and that combination of heat, moisture, and age is exactly the recipe that turns a stable old paint job into a friable lead hazard.

## Local note

One thing we watch for specifically in Pascagoula: homes built on pier-and-beam foundations, common in the older sections of the city, often have painted floor joists and subfloor framing that never get touched during a typical interior repaint. Humidity trapped under these raised foundations means that old lead paint on the framing stays soft and never fully cures, so it sheds dust into the crawl space air at a higher rate than paint on a slab-built house. We test crawl space framing separately from the main structure on these properties rather than assuming the two share the same paint history.
