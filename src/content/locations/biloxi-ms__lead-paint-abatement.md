**Planning a renovation on a Biloxi home built before 1978?** Lead paint abatement isn't just a good idea along the Mississippi Gulf Coast, it's often required before a contractor can touch the walls. Biloxi's housing stock skews old, especially in the neighborhoods closest to the water, and that means lead-based paint is still a live issue every time a wall gets sanded, a window gets replaced, or a porch gets scraped for repainting.

## Why Biloxi Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Much of Biloxi's residential construction predates the 1978 federal ban on lead-based paint, and the Gulf Coast's salt air and humidity speed up the exterior paint failure that exposes those older layers. Coastal homes get repainted more often than inland properties simply because the air eats through finishes faster, and every scrape-and-repaint cycle on a pre-1978 structure is a potential lead exposure event if it isn't handled under EPA RRP (Renovation, Repair, and Painting) protocols. Add in the cycle of storm damage and post-hurricane rebuilding that's shaped so much of Biloxi's housing over the decades, and you get a lot of patched, repainted, and partially renovated homes where nobody documented what was under the top coat.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Biloxi

We start with testing, not assumptions. XRF analysis or lab-submitted paint chip samples tell us which surfaces are actually positive before any containment goes up, because treating every surface as lead-positive wastes a homeowner's money on unnecessary disposal costs. Once we know what we're dealing with, containment is sized to the job: a single window sash gets poly sheeting and a HEPA-vacuum setup, while a full exterior repaint on a historic cottage gets ground-level containment and negative-air equipment to keep dust from drifting onto the next lot over, which matters on Biloxi's tighter coastal lots where houses sit close together. Debris is double-bagged, manifested, and disposed of per EPA lead-waste handling rules, and we finish with clearance dust-wipe sampling before we call a room safe to re-occupy.

## Biloxi Permitting and Renovation Disclosure Rules

Any contractor disturbing more than a few square feet of painted surface in a pre-1978 Biloxi home falls under the federal RRP rule, which requires lead-safe work practices and documented certification, not just good intentions. Harrison County permitting offices expect renovation and demolition permits to reflect that the structure's age triggers this requirement, and sellers and landlords in Biloxi are still bound by the federal disclosure rule that requires buyers and tenants to be told about known lead-based paint before signing. We handle the paperwork side of this alongside the physical work so property owners aren't caught explaining a missed disclosure after a sale or a new lease.

## Local note

One thing that trips up out-of-town contractors working in Biloxi: the combination of high humidity and frequent salt spray means lead paint on exterior siding and porch railings here tends to chalk and flake rather than peel in large sheets, which produces finer, more airborne lead dust during scraping than the paint failure patterns seen inland. We adjust containment and wet-scraping technique accordingly, because dry-scraping chalking paint on a humid Gulf Coast afternoon is exactly how a contained job becomes an uncontained one.
