## Why Bay St. Louis Properties See Reconstruction Needs

Hancock County sits low against the Gulf, and Bay St. Louis carries that exposure directly. Storm surge and wind-driven rain have forced a lot of post-disaster rebuilding here over the past two decades, and the housing stock reflects it: a mix of raised, post-Katrina construction on pilings alongside older slab and crawl-space homes that predate current flood codes. When one of these older homes takes on water or fire damage, reconstruction often has to reconcile original framing with current building requirements, which is a different job than simply replacing drywall. Humidity off the Bay also means exposed framing and subfloor need to come back fast once demolition is done, or secondary moisture problems start before the rebuild even gets underway.

## Our Reconstruction Process in Bay St. Louis

We start with a scope walkthrough that separates what's salvageable from what needs to come out, documenting everything for the insurance file before a single stud is replaced. From there, framing and subfloor repairs happen first, followed by rough-ins for electrical or plumbing if the original systems were compromised by fire, water, or storm damage. Drywall, insulation, and trim come next, matched to the home's existing finish where possible. On raised coastal homes, we pay close attention to flashing and moisture barriers at the pier and beam connections, since that's where a lot of long-term water intrusion starts if it's rebuilt wrong the first time. Final punch-out includes paint, flooring, and fixture reinstallation, with a walkthrough before we consider the job closed.

## Bay St. Louis Insurance & Code Coordination

Most reconstruction jobs here run through a property insurance claim, and we document the original scope of loss, generate repair estimates in formats adjusters recognize, and photograph progress at each phase so the file stays current if the carrier requests updates. Because flood elevation certificates and current wind-zone requirements affect what Hancock County permitting offices will approve for a rebuild, we also factor local code requirements into the repair scope early, rather than discovering a conflict after materials are already ordered. That matters most on older, pre-code homes where a full reconstruction can trigger requirements that a simple repair would not.

## Local note

A lot of Bay St. Louis homes built before current flood maps sit at grade or on short crawl spaces, which means a reconstruction after a flood loss isn't always a like-for-like rebuild. If the home sits in a flood zone that's been remapped since it was built, the permitted scope of work can require raising floor systems or changing subfloor materials to meet current freeboard requirements, something a homeowner doesn't usually find out until the permit application is underway. We flag that possibility during the initial scope walk so there are no surprises mid-project.
