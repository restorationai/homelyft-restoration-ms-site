Many homes here sit on slab or pier foundations built to handle storm surge and sandy soil, and a reconstruction job has to account for that from the framing stage forward, not just patch over what burned or flooded. ## Why Gautier Properties See Reconstruction Needs

Gautier's position along the Gulf Coast means homes take on more than ordinary wear. Salt air corrodes fasteners and flashing faster than it would inland, humidity keeps framing lumber at higher moisture content year round, and tropical storm systems bring both wind and water damage in the same event. A lot of the housing stock mixes older wood-frame construction from before modern wind codes with newer builds done to Mississippi's Fortified standards after storm seasons prompted stricter rebuilding requirements. When a fire or storm forces a full reconstruction, we're often working with both: original framing that needs reinforcing and newer materials that need to match current code, not the code the house was originally built under.

## Our Reconstruction Process in Gautier

Reconstruction starts with documenting what's structurally sound and what isn't, which matters more here because slab foundations can mask water intrusion that's already reached the subfloor or wall base. After demolition of compromised materials, we rebuild framing, subfloor, and drywall to current Jackson County permitting standards, which for storm-damaged structures often means verifying wind-rating requirements on roofing and exterior wall assemblies. For fire damage reconstruction specifically, that includes replacing smoke-compromised framing members, not just surface materials, since char and off-gassing can persist inside studs that look fine from the room side. We coordinate inspections as the county requires them rather than waiting until the end, so there's no rework if an inspector flags something mid-project.

## Gautier Insurance Coordination

Gulf Coast properties frequently carry separate wind and flood policies, and a single loss event, say a hurricane that brings both storm surge and wind-driven roof damage, can mean filing with two carriers for one reconstruction project. We document damage by cause (wind versus water versus fire) as we go, which matters when an adjuster is trying to determine which policy covers which portion of the rebuild. For homes in HOA-governed developments, we also confirm exterior material and color requirements before ordering siding or roofing so the finished reconstruction doesn't trigger a compliance notice after the fact.
