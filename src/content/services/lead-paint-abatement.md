**Peeling paint on a window sash built before 1978 isn't just cosmetic.** Every time that sash moves, it grinds paint chips into dust that settles on sills, floors, and the hands of anyone nearby. If you're planning a renovation, selling a pre-1978 home, or you have a child with elevated blood lead levels and an inspector pointing at the trim, you need someone who can test, contain, and remove the hazard without spreading it through the rest of the house.

## What Lead Paint Abatement actually involves

Lead paint abatement is the permanent removal, enclosure, or encapsulation of lead-based paint hazards, not just a fresh coat over old paint. The work is governed by EPA Renovate, Repair, and Painting (RRP) rules whenever a pre-1978 home is disturbed during a renovation, and by stricter abatement standards when the goal is to eliminate the hazard entirely rather than just manage it during repair work.

Most jobs start with testing, either an XRF reading on suspect surfaces or paint chip samples sent to a lab, because you can't abate what you haven't confirmed. From there the scope depends on the substrate and condition: intact paint on a stable surface might only need encapsulation, while flaking paint on a window system that gets opened and closed usually calls for full removal and replacement of the component. Timelines run from a single day for one room to a week or more for whole-house work tied into a larger renovation.

## Our process

1. **Lead paint testing.** We identify which painted surfaces are lead-positive before any work plan is written, using XRF testing or lab-analyzed samples depending on the scope.
2. **Containment setup.** Poly sheeting, taped-off HVAC returns, and negative air pressure isolate the work area so dust and chips don't migrate into living space.
3. **Work practice selection.** Depending on the surface, we remove, encapsulate, or enclose the hazard using EPA RRP-compliant wet methods, HEPA-vacuum sanding, or component replacement. Dry scraping and uncontained sanding are never part of our approach.
4. **Waste handling and cleanup.** Debris is bagged as lead-contaminated waste and disposed of according to state and federal requirements, and surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and wet-wiped in that order.
5. **Clearance verification.** Dust wipe sampling confirms the area is below EPA clearance levels before we release the space back to you.

## What separates a good lead paint abatement response from a bad one

The most common failure point is skipping the test and assuming paint condition tells the whole story. Intact-looking paint can still fail a dust wipe test if prior sanding or window friction already contaminated the sill and surrounding floor. Another frequent mistake is treating lead work like a standard paint job, opening windows for ventilation and letting dust settle in the yard or drift into an HVAC return, which spreads contamination rather than containing it.

A careful crew documents everything: pre-work photos, containment setup, waste manifests, and post-work clearance results. That paperwork matters if you're closing on a home sale, satisfying a landlord-tenant lead disclosure requirement, or responding to a health department order tied to a child's blood lead test. Rushing containment teardown before clearance results come back is the single biggest shortcut that turns a clean abatement into a callback.

## What does Lead Paint Abatement cost?

Costs vary widely based on how much surface area is affected, whether removal or encapsulation is appropriate, and whether the home needs whole-property testing or just a targeted area.
