If your shingles are curling at the edges, you're finding granules collecting in the gutters after every storm, or a contractor has told you the decking underneath has gone soft, you're past the point where another repair patch makes sense. Gulfport roofs take a beating from salt air, UV exposure, and tropical storm winds, and a roof that's quietly failing from the inside out rarely gives much warning before it leaks. Roofing installation and replacement addresses the whole system, not just the surface layer.

## What Roofing Installation and Replacement actually involves

A full roof replacement means removing the existing roofing material down to the decking, evaluating the plywood or OSB underneath for rot or delamination, and rebuilding the roof system in layers: decking repair where needed, synthetic underlayment, flashing at every penetration and transition, and the final roofing material, whether that's architectural asphalt shingle, standing-seam metal, or another system suited to coastal exposure. On the Mississippi Gulf Coast, that build-out is done with wind uplift resistance in mind, since this region sits inside wind-borne debris and high-velocity hurricane zones where fastening patterns and material ratings actually matter at inspection time. A typical tear-off and replacement on a single-family home runs one to three days depending on roof size, pitch, and whether decking replacement is needed; metal roofing and complex rooflines take longer.

## Our process

1. **Roof and decking inspection.** We walk the roof, check the attic from inside for moisture staining and ventilation issues, and use a moisture meter on suspect decking sections rather than guessing from the ground.
2. **Material and wind-rating selection.** We go over shingle or metal options, the manufacturer's nailing and fastening requirements, and whether the home qualifies for Fortified Roof construction standards, which affects both wind performance and, in many cases, insurance premiums.
3. **Tear-off and decking replacement.** Old roofing comes off down to the deck. Any rotted or delaminated sheathing is replaced before anything new goes down; this step gets skipped by crews in a hurry, and it's the one that causes problems years later.
4. **Underlayment, flashing, and ventilation installation.** Synthetic underlayment, step and counter flashing at walls and chimneys, and proper ridge or soffit ventilation go in before the finish material, since ventilation errors are one of the most common causes of premature shingle failure in humid climates.
5. **Final roofing installation and inspection.** Shingles, metal panels, or the chosen system go on per manufacturer spec, followed by a walk-through and photo documentation for your records and, where applicable, Fortified certification paperwork.

## What separates a good roofing response from a bad one

The difference usually isn't visible from the street. A rushed replacement skips decking inspection, reuses old flashing that's already corroded, or nails shingles outside the manufacturer's wind-rated pattern, which can void the material warranty without the homeowner ever knowing. Insurance adjusters evaluating wind or storm claims look closely at nail placement, flashing condition, and whether ventilation meets code, because these details determine whether a roof failure was pre-existing neglect or storm-caused damage. A roofing contractor working to Fortified standards documents fastener spacing, sealed roof deck, and attachment details as the work happens, not after the fact, which matters if you ever need to support an insurance claim or resell the home with that documentation intact.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Hurricane season runs roughly June through November along the Mississippi coast, and that's when wind uplift and wind-borne debris do the most damage to aging roofs, so spring is the better window for proactive replacement if your roof is already showing wear. Year-round salt air and humidity accelerate corrosion on exposed fasteners and flashing, which is part of why coastal roofing specs call for corrosion-resistant hardware that inland installs don't always require. Summer heat also affects how well asphalt shingles seal down after installation, so timing and technique both factor into how a new roof performs in its first season.
